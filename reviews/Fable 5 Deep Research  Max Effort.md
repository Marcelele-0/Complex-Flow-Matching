# Peer Review — "Cylindrical Flow Matching for Complex-Valued Medical Image Synthesis"
**Venue:** NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Geometric Distributional Deep Learning (GDDL), Paris
**Reviewer role:** Area Chair / Senior Reviewer

---

## 1. Summary
The paper proposes "Cylindrical Flow Matching," a geometry-aware conditional flow-matching scheme that represents a complex MRI signal z = A e^{iθ} on the product manifold M = R⁺ × S¹ with a flat product metric g = dA² + dθ², building geodesic probability paths that interpolate amplitude linearly on R⁺ and phase along the shortest arc on S¹. The stated goal is to avoid the "signal collapse toward the origin" and phase-wrapping artifacts that afflict naive Euclidean (two-channel real/imaginary) flow matching, and the authors demonstrate the idea on 2D SKM-TEA knee slices with a 4× Cartesian reconstruction experiment using exact data-consistency (DC) projections. The core contributions are the manifold representation, an amplitude-weighted L1 phase loss, and a controlled ablation against a Euclidean baseline.

## 2. Strengths
- **On-topic and well-motivated for a geometry workshop.** The problem — phase lives on S¹, and treating C ≅ R² ignores that topology — is real and squarely within the GDDL remit. The observation that linear interpolation of two complex numbers with different phases shrinks the magnitude (via the triangle inequality) is correct and is a clean motivating pathology.
- **Several mathematical claims are correct and cleanly argued.** The bound |u_θ| ≤ π for the S¹ logarithmic map (shortest signed angular difference) is correct. The claim that the Cartesian angular velocity θ̇ = (x u_y − y u_x)/A² = O(A⁻¹) diverges as A → 0 is correct: with x, y = O(A), the numerator is O(A) and the denominator O(A²). The observation that the cylinder is parallelizable with a global frame (∂_A, ∂_θ), enabling direct component-wise velocity regression without moving frames or pushforward operators, is also correct.
- **The base distribution is self-consistent with the chosen metric.** Under g = dA² + dθ² the Riemannian volume form is dA ∧ dθ, so U([0,1]) ⊗ U(S¹) genuinely is the uniform (volume) measure on the cylinder [0,1] × S¹ in the product-metric sense — a nicer property than the paper states, and worth making explicit.
- **The amplitude-weighting instinct is physically sensible.** Down-weighting the phase loss in low-amplitude (background) regions where phase is ill-defined is the right qualitative move, since background MRI phase is essentially uniform noise (Gudbjartsson & Patz, *MRM* 1995, show phase noise becomes near-uniform at SNR ≲ 2).
- **Simulation-free, closed-form conditional velocities.** Because both component geodesics are closed-form, training bypasses ODE simulation, and inference uses a cheap Heun integrator. This is a legitimate practical advantage, albeit one inherited directly from Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, "Flow Matching on General Geometries," ICLR 2024, arXiv:2302.03660: "simulation-free on simple geometries ... computes its target vector field in closed-form").

## 3. Weaknesses & Technical Nitpicks

**3.1 A central geometric claim is simply wrong.** The paper writes that the polar/Euclidean metric g_polar = dA² + A²dθ² ≅ C\{0} has "geodesics [that] are straight lines through the origin." This is incorrect. The metric dA² + A²dθ² is exactly the flat Euclidean metric of R² written in polar coordinates, so (R⁺ × S¹, g_polar) is isometric to the punctured plane, and its geodesics are Euclidean straight lines — but **not through the origin** except in the degenerate collinear cases (equal phase, or phases differing by exactly π). The "signal collapse" the paper invokes is a property of the *linear convex combination* (1−t)z₀ + t z₁ used by Euclidean FM for near-antipodal phases, not a property of the geodesics of g_polar. In fact, for exactly antipodal phases the Euclidean geodesic passes through the removed origin, so a minimizing geodesic in C\{0} does not exist — the opposite of "straight line through the origin." At a geometry-focused venue with expert reviewers, this conflation is a serious blemish and must be corrected.

**3.2 The π₁(M) = Z → "phase seam defects" claim is a non-sequitur.** It is true that π₁(cylinder) = Z. But nontrivial fundamental group does not "cause phase seam defects in flat models." Seam/branch-cut artifacts come from charting S¹ with the interval [−π, π) and the atan2 discontinuity — a coordinate singularity — not from the topology of the manifold. The correct statement is closer to the converse: because π₁(S¹) ≠ 0, no single global smooth coordinate exists, which is *why* a cut appears in any angle parameterization; the intrinsic representation the authors adopt is what removes it. As written, the causal claim is hand-wavy.

**3.3 The "Fisher-information variance reduction weighting" justification is not rigorous — and the exponent is wrong.** For complex Gaussian noise, the phase estimate has standard deviation ≈ σ/A, i.e., phase variance ∝ 1/A² ∝ 1/SNR², and the von Mises concentration that best matches wrapped phase noise scales as κ ≈ A²/σ² ∝ SNR². The Fisher information for phase therefore scales as **A²**, so a genuine inverse-variance / Fisher weighting would use weight ∝ A², not the linear A₁/Ā₁ the paper adopts. The paper's linear weighting corresponds to weighting by SNR (the standard-deviation scale), not by information (the inverse-variance scale). The weighting may still be a reasonable heuristic, but calling it "Fisher-information variance reduction" is incorrect as stated; either change the weight to A² or relabel it as an empirical SNR-proportional heuristic and justify it as such.

**3.4 The optimal-transport "bridge" to Delon et al. (2010) is invoked but not established.** Delon, Salomon & Sobolevski ("Fast transport optimization for Monge costs on the circle," *SIAM J. Appl. Math.* 2010) solve Monge OT for measures *on the circle* S¹, lifting to the universal cover and parameterizing locally optimal plans by a shift/rotation number. The paper's per-pixel conditional geodesic interpolation is not a Monge–Kantorovich OT map between distributions; it is a conditional flow-matching path. Separability of the product cost into an R⁺ part and an S¹ part yields factorized OT **only for product (independent) source and target measures**; real MRI amplitude and phase are not independent (see 3.6), so the separable-OT claim does not transfer. As written, "theoretical bridge to closed-form OT on S¹ via circular CDF shifts" is aspirational and should be softened or actually proved (and the winding-number subtlety of circular OT addressed).

**3.5 Geodesic incompleteness of R⁺ and the positivity of generated amplitudes are under-treated.** R⁺ = (0, ∞) with dA² is geodesically incomplete; a geodesic toward A = 0 reaches the boundary in finite time. The linear path A_t = (1−t)A₀ + t A₁ with A₀, A₁ > 0 does stay positive (convex combination), which the paper correctly relies on for training. But at inference the *learned* velocity field integrated with Heun has no guarantee of staying positive near A → 0; the paper clamps the base distribution with ε = 10⁻⁶ but says nothing about clamping generated trajectories. This is exactly where the O(A⁻¹) pathology the authors cite against the Cartesian formulation could reappear in their own sampler. Please state whether trajectory clamping/reflection is used and quantify how often A_t crosses zero.

**3.6 MRI physics oversights.**
- **Uniform phase prior is unrealistic for foreground tissue.** The 1 − 2/π ≈ 36.3% midpoint attenuation is derived under θ ~ U(S¹) *and* equal amplitudes A₀ = A₁ — a worst case. Real in-vivo phase is spatially smooth, dominated by low-order B0 inhomogeneity and coil-sensitivity phase (background field is "slow varying"/low spatial order); it is approximately uniform only in background noise regions (SNR ≲ 2, where phase noise becomes uniform and magnitude is Rician). The authors even note the empirical attenuation is ~16%. The motivating pathology is thus overstated for the tissue that actually matters.
- **Amplitude and phase are physically coupled, and treating them independently discards structure.** Smooth background phase is tied to coil geometry and B0; even PhaseGen's authors note the generated phase "is also dependent on information not available to the model, such as the coil sensitivity." Treating transport of A and θ as independent ignores this coupling, and the independence assumption also undercuts the OT claim in 3.4.
- **"Single-coil" is a mischaracterization of SKM-TEA.** SKM-TEA was acquired on two 3T GE MR750 scanners with 15/16-channel receiver coils, and raw k-space was collected "in a multi-coil setting" with 2×1 parallel imaging and elliptical sampling (Desai et al., "SKM-TEA," NeurIPS 2021 D&B, arXiv:2203.06823; the GitHub schema lists `# coils` "typically 8 or 16"). The complex "echo1 image space" the paper most likely used is the coil-combined, single-channel complex **SENSE target** (`# maps = 1`, estimated with JSENSE and a 24×24 calibration region) — *not* a single-coil acquisition. Coil-combined SENSE phase differs materially from single-coil phase and from per-coil multi-coil phase. This must be stated precisely, and the phase model's validity discussed accordingly.

**3.7 Statistical analysis is invalid as reported.** "Paired t-test, p < 10⁻⁵ on N = 512 slices" treats adjacent 2D slices as independent, but slices within a volume are strongly correlated (same anatomy, coil profiles, B0). This is pseudoreplication and inflates significance. Worse, the stated dataset is "SKM-TEA-mini," which per the official Hugging Face card (arjundd/skm-tea-mini) contains only 2 training / 1 validation / 1 test scan; 512 test slices therefore come from essentially one subject, so the population-level claim is not supported. Use volume-level aggregation or a mixed-effects model with subject as a random effect, and report the number of distinct held-out volumes. There is also an internal inconsistency: Table 1 says "held-out test volumes" (plural) while the limitations say SKM-TEA-mini (one test scan).

**3.8 Novelty is thin and the closest literature is uncited.** The method is essentially Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman 2024) specialized to the product of a half-line and a circle. Product-manifold generative modeling with component-wise exp/log maps is by now standard: torsional diffusion on the hypertorus (Jing et al., NeurIPS 2022), Riemannian score-based generative models on tori/spheres (De Bortoli et al., NeurIPS 2022), and FoldFlow's explicit product-manifold decomposition where "the exponential and logarithmic maps decompose across each manifold, and the geodesic distance ... is simply the sum of each individual distance" (Bose et al., ICLR 2024). None of these are cited or compared, and none of the paper's geometric machinery is new relative to them.

## 4. Questions for the Authors
1. **Geodesics of g_polar:** Do you agree that straight-line geodesics of the punctured plane do not generically pass through the origin, and that "collapse to the origin" is a property of the Euclidean linear interpolant only for near-antipodal phases? Please restate the geometric argument correctly and clarify what claim survives.
2. **Weighting exponent:** Given that phase variance ∝ 1/A² for complex Gaussian noise (κ ∝ SNR² ∝ A²), why is the phase loss weighted by A rather than A²? Can you provide an ablation over the weight exponent (A⁰, A¹, A²) and reconcile the "Fisher-information" label with the linear choice?
3. **Does the prior actually matter?** With exact DC projection to DC error < 10⁻⁶ at 4× with 24 ACS lines (the low frequencies that carry most energy and the smooth phase), the prior only fills the missing high frequencies. How much of the 0.34 dB / 0.058 SSIM gap over the Euclidean baseline survives if you (a) reduce ACS lines, (b) move to 8×/12×, or (c) evaluate *unconditional* generation quality without DC?
4. **Absolute quality and baselines:** Your ~24.1–24.5 dB PSNR / 0.53–0.59 SSIM on SKM-TEA echo1 is far below published SKM-TEA echo1 (E1) baselines — e.g., CAMP-Net (arXiv:2306.11238), Table II at 6×: U-Net (E1/E2) 31.54 dB / 0.7652 SSIM and Unrolled (E1/E2) 35.03 dB / 0.8296 SSIM, i.e., roughly 6–11 dB higher. What explains this gap (normalization, target = complex SENSE vs. magnitude DICOM, cropping, acceleration), and why is there no empirical comparison to PhaseGen (Rempe et al., arXiv:2504.07560) or to naive fixes (Euclidean FM with phase-unwrapped targets; real/imag prediction with a magnitude–phase loss)?

## 5. Overall Score and Recommendation
**Score: 4/10. Recommendation: Borderline (leaning Reject).**

Judged against a *workshop* bar — where preliminary, work-in-progress results are acceptable and the emphasis is on interesting ideas and geometric framing — the paper is on-topic, identifies a real problem, and proposes a plausible, cheap method. But three factors keep it below the line even for a workshop:

1. **The core geometric argument contains an outright error (§3.1) and a non-sequitur (§3.2)**, which is especially damaging at a differential-geometry-focused venue whose reviewers will catch it immediately.
2. **The novelty is thin (§3.8):** this is Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman 2024) on R⁺ × S¹, a construction already routine in torus/product-manifold generative modeling (torsional diffusion; Riemannian score-based models; FoldFlow), none cited or compared.
3. **The empirical evidence is confounded and under-powered:** DC projection dominates the reconstruction; the numbers are anomalously weak in absolute terms (6–11 dB below standard baselines); the statistics are pseudoreplicated on essentially one subject; and the single baseline that matters, PhaseGen, is absent — as are any unconditional generative-quality metrics (FID/KID on complex data, phase-histogram Wasserstein, NLL, sample diversity).

The ideas are salvageable and the direction is worthwhile, which is why this is a borderline rather than a clear reject. With the corrections and experiments below, a future version could be a strong workshop contribution.

## 6. Actionable Suggestions to Reach 10/10 (Oral / Best-Paper caliber)
1. **Fix and formalize the geometry.** Replace "straight lines through the origin" with the correct statement: (R⁺ × S¹, dA² + A²dθ²) is isometric to the punctured plane; its geodesics are straight segments that hit the removed origin *only* in the antipodal/collinear case, where a minimizing geodesic fails to exist. State a precise proposition: for equal-amplitude endpoints with phase gap Δθ, the Euclidean-interpolant midpoint magnitude is A|cos(Δθ/2)|, giving E[attenuation] = 1 − 2/π under uniform Δθ, whereas your cylindrical path preserves magnitude exactly. This turns a hand-wave into a theorem.
2. **Position against the right literature and prove the specialization is non-trivial.** Explicitly derive Cylindrical FM as the R⁺ × S¹ instance of Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024), and articulate what is genuinely new beyond RFM, torsional diffusion (Jing et al. 2022), Riemannian score-based models (De Bortoli et al. 2022), and FoldFlow's product-manifold decomposition (Bose et al. 2024). If the novelty is the *domain framing plus loss*, defend it, and ideally prove a property (e.g., exact magnitude preservation, or a stability/boundedness guarantee) that generic RFM does not provide.
3. **Correct the loss theory or the exponent.** Either re-derive the weight as A² from the Fisher information of phase under complex Gaussian noise (κ ≈ A²/σ² ∝ SNR²) and validate it, or relabel A-weighting as an empirical heuristic and ablate exponents {0, 1, 2}. Add the ablations the paper itself lacks: trigonometric embedding (A, cos θ, sin θ) vs. raw (A, θ), and the metric choice (cylinder vs. Euclidean vs. log-amplitude cylinder) *holding everything else fixed*, to separate the effect of the embedding from the effect of the geometry.
4. **De-confound the evaluation and add real generative metrics.** Report *unconditional* generation quality on complex data: FID/KID computed on magnitude and on phase separately; a real-vs-synthetic classifier AUROC (as in Schlimbach/Rempe et al., "Generative Modeling of Complex-Valued Brain MRI Data," arXiv:2604.14800, which reports AUROC 0.50–0.66); the flow's NLL/likelihood; and a Wasserstein-on-S¹ distance between generated and real phase histograms. Sweep ACS lines and acceleration (4×/8×/12×) to demonstrate the prior matters once DC is weakened.
5. **Add the baselines and fix the statistics.** Compare against PhaseGen (Rempe et al., arXiv:2504.07560 — "a novel complex-valued diffusion model for generating synthetic MRI raw data conditioned on magnitude images"), Euclidean FM with phase-unwrapped targets, and real/imag prediction with a magnitude–phase loss. Evaluate on the full SKM-TEA reconstruction protocol (many held-out *volumes*, standard 6×/8× Poisson masks) so numbers can be judged against the ~31–35 dB echo1 literature, and reconcile the anomalously low PSNR. Replace the slice-level t-test with a volume-level or mixed-effects analysis and report the number of distinct subjects.
6. **Tighten physics and edge cases.** State explicitly that SKM-TEA is multi-coil (15/16-channel, 3T GE MR750) and that you use coil-combined complex SENSE targets; discuss how coil/B0 phase structure interacts with the amplitude–phase independence assumption. Quantify how often generated A_t crosses zero under Heun and specify the trajectory-level positivity safeguard. Provide architecture, training details (epochs, learning rate, model size, EMA), and code — all currently absent — for reproducibility.

*Note on positioning:* the concurrent April 2026 work Schlimbach/Rempe et al. ("Generative Modeling of Complex-Valued Brain MRI Data," arXiv:2604.14800), which itself cites PhaseGen (arXiv:2504.07560) as reference [16], combines a complex-valued conditional VAE with flow matching to jointly model magnitude and phase. If the authors' submission postdates it, a discussion (not necessarily a head-to-head benchmark) would meaningfully strengthen the novelty positioning.
