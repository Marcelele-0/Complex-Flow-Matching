# Peer Review: "Cylindrical Flow Matching for Complex-Valued Medical Image Synthesis"
**Venue:** NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Geometric Distributional Deep Learning (GDDL), Paris
**Reviewer role:** Area Chair / Senior Reviewer

## 1. Summary
The paper proposes "Cylindrical Flow Matching," a flow-matching generative model for complex-valued MRI that represents a complex signal z = A·e^{iθ} on the product manifold M = R+ × S¹ with product metric g = dA² + dθ², interpolating amplitude linearly and phase along the shortest angular ("geodesic") path, and regresses a 2-channel velocity field (v_A, v_θ) with an amplitude-modulated L1 loss (Eq. 2). The authors argue this "geometry-aware" path avoids the amplitude collapse and phase-seam artifacts they attribute to a Euclidean two-channel baseline, and report a small reconstruction gain (+0.34 dB PSNR, Table 1) on SKM-TEA at 4× Cartesian undersampling with a posterior-sampling reconstruction step. The claimed contributions are the representation/geometry/loss/inference definition, a "controlled ablation" against a Euclidean baseline, and a downstream reconstruction demonstration.

## 2. Strengths
- **The core intuition is sound and well-motivated.** Treating phase as living on S¹ rather than in flat R² is the correct instinct, and the pathology the authors point to — linear interpolation between anti-phase states passing through the origin and attenuating magnitude (Fig. 1a, §1) — is real. A geometry-aware treatment of phase is a legitimate and appropriate topic for a GDDL audience.
- **The problem is genuinely important and timely.** Complex-valued synthesis for k-space augmentation and for validating complex-valued reconstruction networks is an active, under-served niche; the motivation in §1 is credible, and the cited emerging complex-valued literature (Dou et al. 2025, *NMR in Biomedicine* 38(1):e5291 — verified; Rempe et al. 2026, arXiv:2601.18392 — verified to exist) correctly points to a gap.
- **The decoupling of amplitude and phase transport is clean and cheap.** The closed-form target velocity (Eq. 1), simulation-free training, and the O(1) geodesic computation are attractive engineering properties, and the trigonometric input embedding x = (A, cos θ, sin θ) is a reasonable way to avoid feeding a discontinuous angle to the network.
- **The Limitations section is commendably honest** about single-coil simulation, the ε-clamp heuristic, and single-dataset validation — these are the right things to flag, even if (as below) they understate the severity of the problems.

## 3. Weaknesses & Technical Nitpicks
This is the longest section because the paper's central claims do not survive scrutiny. I organize the critique into four blocks.

### 3.1 Mathematical / geometric issues
**(a) The "isometric polar embedding" claim is mathematically wrong (§2).** The paper writes that g_polar = dA² + A²dθ² is "≅ C\{0}, whose geodesics are straight lines through the origin." g_polar is indeed the flat Euclidean metric of R²\{0} written in polar coordinates, so its geodesics are straight lines *in the plane* — but they are emphatically **not** all "through the origin." Only purely radial geodesics pass through the origin; a generic straight-line segment between two off-axis points does not. This is exactly the interpolation path the authors are (correctly) complaining about, but the geometric statement as written is false and should be corrected.

**(b) The product metric is not a geometry of the physical signal space — this is the paper's deepest problem.** The chosen metric g = dA² + dθ² makes M a flat cylinder of **fixed radius 1**, independent of A. This is *not* isometric to C ≅ R² (the physical signal space); the "geodesic" phase interpolation θ_t is therefore not a geodesic in the signal space at all. Stripped of the geometric vocabulary, the method is exactly: (i) linear interpolation of amplitude, A_t = (1−t)A₀ + tA₁, and (ii) constant-speed angular interpolation (SLERP) of phase, θ_t = θ₀ + t·atan2(sin Δθ, cos Δθ). No curvature, no parallel transport, no nontrivial connection, and no Riemannian exponential map beyond the trivial 1-D circle case is ever actually used. For a workshop on differential geometry and OT, the "Riemannian machinery" is essentially decorative: this is a coordinate reparameterization (Cartesian → polar with an angle chart), not a genuinely geometric flow. This must be owned honestly or replaced by a metric/connection that does non-trivial work. Note that Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman, ICLR 2024) — which the paper itself cites as its "manifold extension" — is explicitly *simulation-free on simple geometries* and its premetric construction "encompasses the existing Euclidean case"; the flat product cylinder is precisely the trivial regime RFM already covers, so the construction is not a new manifold capability.

**(c) The cut locus / discontinuity of the target field is unaddressed and undercuts the central "geometry-aware" claim.** On S¹ with the shortest-path convention, antipodal points (Δθ = ±π) form a cut locus: the shortest geodesic is non-unique, and u_θ = atan2(sin Δθ, cos Δθ) is **discontinuous** as a function of (θ₀, θ₁) across Δθ = π (jumping between +π and −π; atan2 returns +π there by convention). Thus the regression target the network must fit is itself discontinuous on a measure-zero set — precisely the class of pathology the paper accuses the Euclidean baseline of. The claim that the formulation "natively resolves phase discontinuities" (Abstract, §1, §2.2) is therefore overstated: it relocates the discontinuity from the representation to the target vector field. This must be acknowledged and, ideally, analyzed and quantified.

**(d) The "unbounded angular velocity" argument against the baseline is a strawman (§2).** The paper argues that under Cartesian flow the induced θ̇ = (x·u_y − y·u_x)/A² = O(A⁻¹) diverges as A → 0. The dimensional analysis is correct (numerator ~ A·u, denominator ~ A², giving O(A⁻¹)). **But the Cartesian baseline never regresses θ̇** — it regresses the Cartesian velocity (u_x, u_y), which is perfectly bounded and well-defined at the origin. Criticizing the divergence of a derived quantity that the competing method never computes is not a fair comparison. The genuine (and much narrower) statement is that *post-hoc* angular-velocity read-out from the Cartesian field is ill-conditioned in low-SNR background — a statement about phase estimation, not about the baseline's numerical stability.

**(e) The claimed midpoint-attenuation closed form holds only in a special case (§1).** The arithmetic is correct: for Δθ ~ U(−π,π), E|cos(Δθ/2)| = (1/2π)∫_{−π}^{π}|cos(Δθ/2)| dΔθ = 2/π ≈ 0.6366, so attenuation = 1 − 2/π ≈ 36.3%. **However**, the identity |z_{1/2}| = A·|cos(Δθ/2)| requires **equal amplitudes** |z₀| = |z₁| = A. The paper's own prior is p₀ = U([0,1]) ⊗ U(S¹) while the target is real MRI data, so A₀ ≠ A₁ in general and the closed form does not apply. The honest quantity is E[|(1−t)z₀ + tz₁|] for general amplitudes; the stated 36.3% should be presented as a special-case illustration, not as the operative expected attenuation.

**(f) The Fisher-information justification for the loss weight is incorrect (§2.2).** The loss weights the phase term by (A₁/Ā₁) — i.e., **linearly** in amplitude — and calls this "a Fisher-information variance reduction weighting." For a complex measurement A·e^{iθ} + n with n complex Gaussian of variance σ², the Fisher information for the phase θ is **A²/σ² (i.e., ∝ SNR², scaling as A²)**, and the Cramér–Rao bound for phase is var(θ̂) ≥ σ²/A² (up to sample-count constants; cf. the standard CRLB for phase of a sinusoid in AWGN, var(φ̂) ≥ 2σ²/(N·A²)). The Fisher-information-optimal weighting is therefore ∝ A², **not** A¹. As written, the appeal to Fisher information is wrong by a full power of A. Either weight by A² and justify it, or drop the statistical claim and present the linear weight as a heuristic.

**(g) Output-side chart dependence contradicts the input-side continuity fix (§2.1).** The network ingests a continuous embedding (A, cos θ, sin θ) but predicts a *scalar* v_θ in a chart on S¹. Predicting a scalar tangent coordinate reintroduces a chart/branch-cut dependence on the **output** side that the trigonometric input was designed to remove, and the map is not manifestly equivariant to the S¹ action θ → θ + φ. A consistent construction would predict a tangent vector in the embedded (cos θ, sin θ) representation and project onto the tangent line. This inconsistency is worth raising explicitly.

**(h) The OT rhetoric is unbacked by the method (§2).** The paper invokes "a natural theoretical bridge to closed-form Optimal Transport on S¹ via circular CDF shifts" and cites Delon et al., but the actual coupling is the **independent** product p₀ = U([0,1]) ⊗ U(S¹) with independently sampled endpoints — which is precisely **not** an optimal-transport coupling. For a workshop centered on OT, name-dropping circular OT while implementing independent coupling is a substantive gap between rhetoric and method, and should either be closed (implement the circular-CDF OT coupling) or removed.

### 3.2 Empirical issues
**(a) The headline reconstruction numbers are alarmingly low, not competitive.** Table 1 reports Cylindrical PSNR = 24.47 dB / SSIM = 0.588 at 4× — with **exact data consistency** enforced (25% of k-space measured, including a 24-line ACS band). For context: a plain zero-filled reconstruction of 4× knee data sits around 24.5 dB (fastMRI random 4× zero-filling = 24.5 ± 1.37 dB, MTrans, arXiv:2106.14248); a single-coil U-Net reaches ~31.9 dB (fastMRI single-coil knee 4×, PC-RNN, arXiv:1912.00543); and on SKM-TEA's own Raw Data Track the U-Net baseline reaches **31.54 dB (E1)** and unrolled networks **35.03 dB (E1)** even at the harder 6× setting (CAMP-Net Table II, arXiv:2306.11238). A generative prior with exact 4× data consistency that lands at ~24.5 dB is performing at roughly zero-filled level — i.e., the prior is contributing little or nothing beyond the measured data. This is a red flag that the pipeline (normalization, coil handling, or the prior itself) is broken, and it badly undermines the "downstream value" claim.

**(b) The improvement is marginal and the significance claim is fragile.** The Table 1 gain is +0.34 dB PSNR with SDs of ±1.18 and ±1.16 dB — the effect is ~0.3σ, i.e., practically negligible. The p < 10⁻⁵ (paired t-test, N = 512 slices) almost certainly reflects **pseudoreplication**: 512 slices from few volumes are highly correlated (adjacent SKM-TEA slices are near-duplicates — one recent work deliberately keeps only every 8th slice for this reason), so slice-level t-tests dramatically overstate significance. Statistics should be computed at the volume level (or with a mixed-effects model), and the number of independent volumes must be reported.

**(c) Figure 2 vs. Table 1 is internally inconsistent and looks cherry-picked.** Figure 2's "representative" slice shows Cylindrical 28.39 dB / 0.7624 vs. Euclidean 25.35 dB / 0.6469 — a **+3.04 dB** gap — while Table 1's average gap is **+0.34 dB**, roughly a factor of nine smaller. A "representative" example showing ~9× the mean effect is, by definition, not representative. Additionally the figure labels the phase metric "GPE" while the text calls it "CPE" (Eq. 3) — inconsistent naming for what appears to be the same quantity.

**(d) The "~16% empirical attenuation" number is unsubstantiated (§1).** The text asserts empirical midpoint attenuation of "~16% on spatially correlated MRI data" versus the 36.3% theoretical figure, but no experiment, table, or appendix supports it. Either show the measurement or remove the number.

**(e) Only one baseline, and it is a self-constructed foil.** The sole comparison is against a "Euclidean flow" trained in the authors' own pipeline. There is no comparison to PhaseGen (Rempe et al. 2025 — a directly relevant complex-valued MRI diffusion model with public code at github.com/TIO-IKIM/PhaseGen), to score-based MRI diffusion (Chung & Ye 2022), to Riemannian Flow Matching applied directly (Chen & Lipman 2024), to a standard two-channel U-Net, to compressed sensing (CS-TV), or even to zero-filling. For an empirical claim of a "geometrically grounded baseline," this is insufficient.

**(f) The evidence type does not match the claimed contribution.** The paper's central claim is about **synthesis** (generating complex-valued data), yet it reports only **reconstruction** metrics (PSNR/SSIM/NMSE/CPE). There is no FID, no complex-domain distributional metric, and no downstream augmentation experiment. Contrast PhaseGen, which demonstrated that training with its synthetic phase raised k-space skull-stripping from a Dice of 41.1% (naive phase) to **80.1%** (arXiv:2504.07560) — exactly the kind of synthesis-utility evidence this paper needs and lacks. The synthesis contribution is essentially unvalidated.

**(g) "Controlled ablations" are claimed but absent.** Contribution (2) promises controlled ablations, but there is no ablation of λ, of the amplitude modulation, of the trigonometric embedding, of the Heun NFE, or of linear vs. log amplitude. The single Euclidean-vs-cylindrical contrast is not an ablation study.

**(h) Reproducibility is essentially nil.** No architecture, no hyperparameters (beyond λ = 1.0 and ε = 10⁻⁶), no training details, no inference NFE, no compute/runtime, no seeds, no code statement. The posterior-sampling reconstruction is asserted in one sentence with no algorithm, no named likelihood approximation (DPS? ΠGDM? projection?), and no step count.

### 3.3 MRI physics issues
**(a) The dataset/anatomy/coil description is internally contradictory and likely fatal to a phase paper.** SKM-TEA (Desai et al., NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks; arXiv:2203.06823) is a **multi-coil (15/16-channel)** 3D quantitative DESS **knee** dataset (155 scans, ~25,000 slices, 1.6 TB, 3 T GE MR750) providing raw k-space, JSENSE/ESPIRiT sensitivity maps, and complex SENSE reconstructions. The paper claims "single-coil, first-echo image space." SKM-TEA ships **no** native single-coil data; a single-coil signal must be *derived* — and the standard derivation, root-sum-of-squares (RSS) coil combination, is **magnitude-only and destroys phase**. Any non-phase-preserving combination would make the phase the paper claims to model an artifact of the combination rather than tissue phase, collapsing the paper's entire premise. The authors must state exactly how the complex single-coil signal was obtained (SENSE with a reference map? a single selected coil element?), because the validity of everything downstream hinges on it. Separately, the text and Fig. 2 describe "2D axial slices," but SKM-TEA's raw-data track reconstructs 2D slices "in the (ky, kz) plane" (SKM-TEA DATASET.md) of a **sagittal** qDESS acquisition, and the dominant convention plus the dataset's own sagittal-plane segmentations treat the standard reconstructed slices as **sagittal**, not axial. (I note a terminology trap: some sources use "axial" to mean the readout/slice-stacking direction, and the SKM-TEA authors' ISMRM abstract loosely says "axial slices" — but the in-plane reconstructed slice is sagittal.) The apparent brain-like/non-knee slice in Fig. 2 combined with the "axial" label needs to be resolved; as presented it suggests either a wrong figure or a misdescribed pipeline.

**(b) "SKM-TEA-mini" (Limitations) appears not to be an official subset.** I can find no official SKM-TEA "mini" release. If it is an ad hoc subset, say so and define it (number of volumes, selection criterion).

**(c) Phase in MRI is largely not an intrinsic tissue property.** Measured phase is dominated by B0 inhomogeneity, coil-sensitivity phase, eddy currents, chemical shift, receiver phase offsets, and gradient delays; the ISMRM QSM consensus documents the coil phase as φ^c = φ_B(TE) + φ₀^c, where φ₀^c bundles transmit-field phase, conductivity, gradient delays, eddy currents, and per-coil sensitivity. Generating "phase" without conditioning on field maps or coil sensitivities produces phase that is not physically meaningful as tissue phase — a serious problem for a paper whose entire premise is faithful phase synthesis. This is unaddressed.

**(d) "Resolving the ±π boundary" is conflated with physical phase wrapping.** Resolving the atan2 branch cut in a generative interpolation is not the same problem as physical phase unwrapping (an inverse problem over spatial phase gradients). The paper's language elides this distinction.

**(e) Conjugate-symmetry / partial-Fourier interplay is unexamined.** For a purely real object, k-space is Hermitian; phase breaks this, which is exactly why clinical partial-Fourier acquires slightly more than half of k-space (5/8–7/8) and applies phase-corrected homodyne/POCS reconstruction. A claim that complex synthesis aids k-space augmentation should engage with how synthesized phase interacts with the Hermitian/partial-Fourier structure that real reconstructions exploit.

**(f) Single-coil simulation weakens the downstream claim.** Real accelerated MRI is parallel imaging (SENSE/GRAPPA/ESPIRiT). A single-coil retrospective simulation cannot support claims about "downstream reconstruction" in realistic settings; the authors partly acknowledge this but still frame the reconstruction as evidence of practical value. Note too that a single-coil signal derived from a coil-combined image may already have had its phase altered by the combination.

**(g) Exact data consistency confounds the comparison.** With DC enforced exactly on 25% of k-space (DC error < 10⁻⁶), much of the reported metric is attributable to the measured data, not the prior. An essential missing control is a **null/random prior + identical DC** to isolate the prior's contribution — especially given that the absolute PSNR (§3.2a) is near zero-filled level.

**(h) The 5%-of-peak tissue mask for CPE (Eq. 3) is threshold-sensitive and its provenance matters.** The mask must be computed from the **ground truth** for both methods (not from each method's own reconstruction) to be fair; the paper does not say which. A sensitivity analysis over the threshold is warranted.

### 3.4 Citation, positioning, and presentation issues
**(a) Miscitation of Delon et al. — a plain factual error.** The paper cites "Delon, Salomon, Sobolevski, *Fast optimal transport on the circle*, SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis, 47(6):4313–4335, 2010." The correct reference is **"Fast transport optimization for Monge costs on the circle," SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics, 70(7):2239–2258, 2010** (doi:10.1137/090772708; arXiv:0902.3527). The title, the journal, the volume, the issue, and the page range are **all wrong**. This is especially unfortunate given that circular OT is the paper's one link to the workshop's OT theme.

**(b) Mischaracterization of Chung & Ye (2022).** The paper cites Chung & Ye as an example of "naively treating the complex signal as a flat, two-channel tensor." In fact, Chung & Ye **train on magnitude images only** and reconstruct complex-valued data via alternating SDE-solver and data-consistency steps, extending to parallel imaging ("Our model requires magnitude images only for training, and yet is able to reconstruct complex-valued data"). Using it as the exemplar of naïve two-channel complex modeling is a misreading. (The metadata — Med. Image Anal. 80:102479, 2022 — is otherwise correct.)

**(c) Category error with DreaMR (Bedel & Çukur), plus a wrong DOI.** DreaMR is a counterfactual-explanation method for **functional MRI / BOLD time series**, not complex k-space or complex images. Invoking it in a complex-image/k-space topology argument (§1) is a category error. Moreover, the cited DOI 10.1109/TMI.2024.3475877 is incorrect; the correct record is **doi:10.1109/TMI.2024.3507008**.

**(d) Unfair dismissal of PhaseGen.** PhaseGen (Rempe et al. 2025, arXiv:2504.07560) is the closest prior art — a complex-valued diffusion model that generates complex MRI raw data conditioned on magnitude, using complex-valued noise N_z = e^{iε}, ε ~ U(−π,π), with public code and quantitative downstream results. Dismissing it in one clause for "lack of explicit phase topology preservation," with no numerical comparison, is inadequate; it should be a baseline.

**(e) The novelty claim is weak against established angular/manifold generative modeling.** Flow matching and diffusion on the circle/torus are well-established prior art: Riemannian Flow Matching (Chen & Lipman 2024) explicitly covers the flat/product and simulation-free case that subsumes the cylinder; Riemannian Score-Based Generative Modelling (De Bortoli et al. 2022) handles the torus and sphere and even discusses seam artifacts of extrinsic/wrapped approaches; and Torsional Diffusion (Jing et al. 2022) does generative modeling on the hypertorus of angles with an SE(3)-equivariant score. The cylinder R+ × S¹ with a product metric is the simplest possible non-Euclidean example, and the paper does not differentiate its contribution from this body of work. "Flow matching on S¹" is not itself novel, and the review must say so.

**(f) Overclaiming language throughout.** "Natively resolves," "guaranteeing numerical stability," "establishing a geometrically grounded baseline," and the Abstract's "prevents phase artifacts and signal collapse" are not supported by a +0.34 dB result. The atan2 wrapped-difference (Eq. 1) is presented with more ceremony ("S¹ logarithmic map") than a signed angular difference deserves.

**(g) Venue-footer mismatch.** The manuscript footer says "Submitted to 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)" (main track), but it is under review for the GDDL workshop. Minor, but should be fixed for camera-ready and could constitute a dual-submission/formatting issue if the main-track version is live.

**(h) Undefined key quantity.** The paper never defines what "amplitude" means for a coil-combined complex image, nor states whether Fig. 1(a)'s antipodal example is representative or a worst case (it is the worst case).

## 4. Questions for the Authors
1. **Coil combination and phase provenance:** Exactly how did you obtain a "single-coil, complex, first-echo image" from SKM-TEA's 15/16-channel raw k-space? If you used RSS, the phase is destroyed and the entire premise collapses; if you used SENSE/ESPIRiT with a reference map, the phase you model is coil-sensitivity- and B0-contaminated rather than tissue phase. Please specify the exact operation and justify that the modeled phase is physically meaningful. Relatedly, is Fig. 2 a knee slice, and why is it described as "axial" when SKM-TEA reconstructs in the sagittal (ky, kz) plane?
2. **Absolute performance:** Your 4× reconstruction with *exact* data consistency reaches 24.47 dB PSNR — roughly the level of zero-filling (~24.5 dB), and ~7 dB below a standard U-Net on comparable knee data (~31.5 dB E1 even at 6× on SKM-TEA). What accounts for this? Please add a "null prior + identical DC" control so reviewers can see whether the generative prior contributes anything at all.
3. **Geometry vs. reparameterization:** Beyond linear interpolation on A and SLERP on θ, what specific Riemannian structure (curvature, connection, non-trivial exponential map) does your fixed-radius product metric g = dA² + dθ² contribute that a plain polar-coordinate reparameterization does not — and that RFM (Chen & Lipman 2024) does not already provide for free? How do you handle the discontinuity of u_θ across the antipodal cut locus, given the target field is non-continuous there?
4. **Statistics and synthesis evidence:** How many independent volumes underlie the N = 512 slices, and does the +0.34 dB gain survive a volume-level (or mixed-effects) test? Since your central contribution is synthesis, why are there no distributional/generative metrics (e.g., FID or a complex-domain analogue) and no augmentation experiment against PhaseGen (which showed a 41.1% → 80.1% downstream Dice gain from synthetic phase)?

## 5. Overall Score and Recommendation
**Score: 3 / 10 — Reject.**

The paper is built on a correct and appealing intuition (phase lives on S¹), and the writing is clear, but the execution does not hold up. The geometric contribution reduces to a polar reparameterization dressed in Riemannian language on the simplest possible non-flat manifold, with prior art (RFM, RSGM, torsional diffusion) that already covers circular/toroidal generative modeling far more deeply. The mathematics contains at least one outright error (geodesics "through the origin"), one incorrect statistical justification (Fisher information ∝ A², not A¹), an unaddressed cut-locus discontinuity that directly contradicts the "natively resolves discontinuities" claim, and a strawman stability argument. The empirical section reports near-zero-filled absolute PSNR with a negligible +0.34 dB gain whose significance rests on pseudoreplicated slice statistics, a cherry-picked figure inconsistent with the table by ~9×, only one self-built baseline, no synthesis metrics despite synthesis being the headline, and no ablations despite claiming them as a contribution. The MRI-physics foundation is shaky: a phase-modeling paper that may have derived its data via phase-destroying coil combination, on a dataset it mislabels ("axial" knee vs. sagittal SKM-TEA), with a demonstrably wrong key citation and a category-error citation (DreaMR/fMRI). Individually, several of these would be fixable in revision; collectively they indicate the work is not ready for a rigorous geometric-DL audience. I recommend rejection, with genuine encouragement to resubmit a substantially strengthened version — the topic is worthwhile.

## 6. Actionable Suggestions to Reach 10/10
Concrete, implementable changes, in roughly descending priority:
1. **Fix the data pipeline and prove phase validity.** State the exact coil-combination method; if phase was destroyed by RSS, redo using the provided ESPIRiT/JSENSE maps or a single physical coil element, and demonstrate that the modeled phase corresponds to tissue phase (e.g., compare against the dataset's complex SENSE reference). Correct the "axial" → sagittal description and confirm the anatomy in Fig. 2 is knee.
2. **Add the decisive control and real baselines.** Report in the identical pipeline: (i) zero-filled, (ii) two-channel U-Net, (iii) CS-TV, (iv) Chung & Ye score-MRI, (v) RFM applied directly to R+×S¹, and (vi) PhaseGen. Add a "null/random prior + identical DC" ablation to isolate the prior's contribution. Given the current absolute numbers, this is essential to show the method does anything.
3. **Match evidence to the synthesis claim.** Report generative-quality metrics (FID or a complex-domain distributional distance) and a concrete augmentation experiment (e.g., does synthetic complex data improve a k-space classifier/segmenter, à la PhaseGen's 41.1% → 80.1% skull-stripping result).
4. **Repair the mathematics.** (a) Correct the geodesic statement (straight lines in the plane, not "through the origin"). (b) Replace the Fisher-information claim: derive the CRLB for phase (var(θ̂) ≥ σ²/A², i.e., information ∝ A²) and either weight by A² and justify it, or relabel the linear weight an empirical heuristic. (c) Restate the attenuation result as E[|(1−t)z₀+tz₁|] for general amplitudes, presenting 1−2/π ≈ 36.3% as the equal-amplitude special case. (d) Add an explicit treatment of the antipodal cut locus and the discontinuity of u_θ — ideally a smoothing or stochastic tie-breaking scheme — and quantify how often Δθ ≈ ±π occurs in the data.
5. **Make the geometry do real work.** Either (a) adopt the amplitude-dependent metric g = dA² + A²dθ² (the true signal-space metric) and derive the correct geodesics — turning the paper into a genuine Riemannian contribution and directly testing whether physical-space geodesics beat the fixed-radius cylinder — or (b) implement the circular-OT coupling of Delon et al. that you advertise, so the OT claim is actually backed. Predict the phase velocity as a tangent vector in the embedded (cos θ, sin θ) frame to remove output-side chart dependence.
6. **Fix statistics and reporting.** Report the number of independent volumes; run volume-level or mixed-effects significance tests; replace the cherry-picked Fig. 2 with a slice at the median gain (and show the full distribution of per-slice gains). Reconcile the figure metrics with Table 1 and unify GPE/CPE naming.
7. **Deliver the promised ablations (Contribution 2):** λ sweep, amplitude-modulation on/off, trigonometric embedding on/off, Heun NFE, linear vs. log amplitude.
8. **Full reproducibility:** architecture, hyperparameters, training/inference details, NFE, runtime/compute, seeds, and a code-availability statement; specify the posterior-sampling algorithm (DPS/ΠGDM/projection), its likelihood approximation, and step count.
9. **Correct all citations and positioning.** Fix the Delon et al. reference (SIAM J. Appl. Math., 70(7):2239–2258, 2010, doi:10.1137/090772708); correct the DreaMR DOI (10.1109/TMI.2024.3507008) and remove or re-scope the DreaMR (fMRI) and Chung & Ye (magnitude-trained) mischaracterizations; add a positioning paragraph distinguishing the work from RFM (Chen & Lipman 2024), RSGM (De Bortoli et al. 2022), and Torsional Diffusion (Jing et al. 2022). Temper the overclaiming language throughout, and fix the NeurIPS-main-track footer.
10. **Physics conditioning:** condition synthesis on field maps and/or coil sensitivities so generated phase is physically plausible, and discuss the conjugate-symmetry/partial-Fourier implications of injecting synthetic phase into k-space augmentation.
