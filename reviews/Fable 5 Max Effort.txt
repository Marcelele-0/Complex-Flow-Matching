Review: "Cylindrical Flow Matching for Complex-Valued Medical Image Synthesis"

Venue: NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Geometric Distributional Deep Learning (GDDL) · Reviewer role: Area Chair / Senior Reviewer

1. Summary

The paper proposes performing flow matching on the product manifold M = ℝ⁺ × S¹ with metric g = dA² + dθ², rather than on C ≅ ℝ², for generating complex-valued MRI data. Conditional probability paths decouple into linear amplitude interpolation and wrapped angular geodesics, yielding closed-form, globally bounded target velocities (|u_θ| ≤ π) and simulation-free training; an amplitude-weighted L1 loss suppresses supervision of ill-defined background phase. On single-coil SKM-TEA slices, the model used as a prior for 4× Cartesian posterior-sampling reconstruction with exact data consistency improves PSNR/SSIM/NMSE and a circular phase error metric over a Euclidean flow-matching baseline.

2. Strengths
Well-chosen problem, squarely in scope. Complex-valued/phase-aware generative modeling for MRI is genuinely underexplored, and the paper connects Riemannian flow matching, circular OT, and a real physical signal domain — exactly the workshop's remit.
The core quantitative motivation is correct. I verified the analytic claim: under an independent uniform phase prior, expected midpoint attenuation is 1 − E|cos(Δθ/2)| = 1 − 2/π ≈ 36.3% (L38–40). The induced angular velocity under Cartesian flow, θ̇ = (xu_y − yu_x)/A², is indeed O(A⁻¹) near the origin, while the cylindrical target is bounded by π (L69–72). Bounded, low-variance regression targets are a real optimization advantage, cleanly argued.
Correct, economical use of the RFM machinery. Exploiting parallelizability of the cylinder to avoid pushforward operators, closed-form geodesics to keep training simulation-free, and the (A, cos θ, sin θ) embedding to eliminate the input branch cut (§2.1) are all sound engineering choices, clearly presented for a 4-page format.
Honest limitations section and an appropriate wrapped-error metric (Eq. 3); significance testing is at least attempted; Figure 2's error-map layout is informative.
3. Weaknesses & Technical Nitpicks

(a) The "intrinsic manifold" claim is an overclaim (L59–65). The chosen metric dA² + dθ² is not the intrinsic geometry of C — that is precisely the polar metric dA² + A²dθ² the authors discard. What is actually proposed is a preferred coordinate system plus a non-isometric product metric with mixed units (signal amplitude vs. radians), which silently fixes a "cylinder radius" scale of 1. Equivalently, the relative amplitude/phase transport cost is an untuned hyperparameter (λ = 1.0 in Eq. 2, never ablated). Moreover, the true signal space including zeros is the cone C (contractible); ℝ⁺ × S¹ is its oriented real blow-up at the origin. The claimed "intrinsic topology S¹" holds only away from A = 0 — exactly the region where the authors must patch things with the ε-clamp and amplitude-weighted loss.

(b) Conditional-path pathology ≠ marginal/model failure. The 36.3% attenuation is a property of the conditional interpolants under independent coupling; the learned marginal vector field averages over conditionals and need not collapse amplitude. The paper never disentangles whether gains come from (i) topology-respecting paths, (ii) lower target variance, or (iii) the coordinate parameterization itself. This is the key scientific question for a geometry workshop, and the single-baseline ablation cannot answer it.

(c) The baseline is underspecified — this is the crux. Is "Euclidean Flow (ℝ²)" a two-channel (Re, Im) model or a flat (A, θ) model? A (Re, Im) model has no branch cut by construction, so the "severe phase seam errors" in Fig. 2(b) are mechanistically puzzling; if instead the baseline interpolates (A, θ) as flat channels, the comparison conflates two distinct failure modes (origin-crossing vs. seam artifacts) and a (Re, Im) baseline becomes mandatory. Lines 34–42 describe both pathologies interchangeably. Relatedly, the fundamental-group sentence (L66–67) is logically garbled: π₁(M) = Z is a property of the cylinder; flat ℝ² is simply connected, so "non-trivial fundamental group... causes phase seam defects in flat models" does not parse as written.

(d) Loose or incorrect statements.

L65: "whose geodesics are straight lines through the origin" — Euclidean geodesics are straight lines, generically not through the origin. Only antipodal-phase chords approach it.
L87–89: The "Fisher-information variance reduction weighting" claim is unsupported. Under additive complex Gaussian noise, the phase CRLB gives Fisher information ∝ A²/σ², so a principled weighting is quadratic in A, not linear; the choice of endpoint A₁ rather than A_t is also unjustified.
L40: "empirically ∼16% on spatially correlated MRI data" — no measurement protocol is given.
L72–73: The "natural theoretical bridge to closed-form OT on S¹" is invoked but never used (the coupling appears to be independent). Also, a separable cost yields a product-of-1D-OT plans argument only for product marginals; MRI amplitude and phase are dependent, so the claimed bridge needs a caveat.

(e) Cut locus unaddressed. With independent uniform coupling, u_θ is discontinuous at Δθ = ±π; targets near the cut locus have maximal variance and flip sign. Standard issue for S¹ flows, unexamined here.

(f) Boundary behavior at inference. Nothing prevents the Heun-integrated learned field from driving A < 0; ℝ⁺ is not geodesically complete under this metric. Is A clamped or reflected during sampling?

(g) Experimental credibility issues. Figure 2's representative slice shows +3.04 dB PSNR and +0.116 SSIM, while Table 1's means show +0.34 dB and +0.058 — an order-of-magnitude gap that invites cherry-picking concerns; the figure also reports "GPE" while Eq. 3 defines "CPE." The paired t-test over N = 512 slices ignores within-volume correlation (slices from one subject are not independent). No external anchors (zero-filled, ℓ1-wavelet CS, a score-based baseline such as Chung & Ye, or PhaseGen) contextualize the absolute numbers, and SSIM ≈ 0.53–0.59 at 4× with a 24-line ACS and exact DC is low enough to question baseline strength. Contribution 2 promises ablations on generation, but every reported metric is a DC-constrained reconstruction metric — no unconditional generation statistics appear. The posterior sampler itself is unspecified (L115–117): how is the complex-domain k-space projection interleaved with an ODE evolving in (A, θ) coordinates? Finally: no architecture, parameter count, NFE, training details, amplitude normalization scheme (the base is U([0,1]) — is data rescaled?), and "SKM-TEA" (L99) vs. "SKM-TEA-mini" (L128) is inconsistent.

(h) MRI physics. Single-coil, coil-combined "image space" phase is typically dominated by smooth background/coil-sensitivity phase rather than anatomically meaningful phase — does synthesizing it actually stress the stated use case (validating complex-valued k-space networks, L23–26)? The k-space leakage motivation (L31–33) is never tested with any k-space-domain metric, and masking CPE at 5% amplitude hides background phase that still contaminates all of k-space through the FFT.

4. Questions for the Authors
Baseline specification. Is the Euclidean baseline (Re, Im) or flat (A, θ)? If (Re, Im), give the mechanistic explanation for "phase seam" artifacts in Fig. 2(b), since that parameterization has no branch cut; if flat (A, θ), will you add the (Re, Im) baseline, without which the topology claim is untested?
Metric scale and the origin. How sensitive are results to the implicit amplitude/phase scale (equivalently λ, or g_ρ = dA² + ρ²dθ²)? And since the true signal space is the cone C rather than its blow-up, how does the model behave in near-zero-amplitude regions — does generated uniform background phase distort k-space statistics relative to real data?
Cut locus and coupling. Did you observe target-variance or instability effects near Δθ = ±π? Given you explicitly cite Delon et al., why not use minibatch circular-OT coupling, which would both remove the cut-locus discontinuity and substantiate the claimed OT bridge?
Reconstruction protocol and statistics. Precisely which posterior sampling algorithm is used, and how is the linear complex-domain DC projection composed with the cylindrical-coordinate ODE at each step? How representative is Fig. 2 given the 3 dB vs. 0.34 dB gap with Table 1, are the ± values per-slice std, and does significance survive per-volume clustering?
5. Overall Score and Recommendation

Score: 5/10 — Borderline. The core idea is sound, the geometry is (mostly) correctly executed, and the fit to this workshop is excellent. But the baseline ambiguity (c), the Figure-2/Table-1 inconsistency (g), the conflation of conditional-path pathology with model failure (b), and the fact that the paper's own stated motivation — enabling training/validation of complex-valued networks — is never actually tested, together keep this below a clear accept. I would raise to 6–7 (poster accept) given a convincing rebuttal on Q1 and Q4.

6. Actionable Suggestions to Reach 10/10 (Oral / Best-Paper Caliber)
Run the full disentangling ablation matrix: {(Re, Im), flat (A, θ), cylindrical} × {independent coupling, minibatch OT / circular-OT (Delon)}. This isolates coordinates vs. metric vs. coupling and would turn the central claim from plausible into demonstrated. Report unconditional generation metrics too: phase-gradient histograms, radial k-space power spectra, and a complex-domain distributional distance.
Add the killer experiment the introduction promises: train-synthetic-test-real (TSTR). Train a complex-valued reconstruction or denoising network (in the spirit of the Dou/Rempe lines you cite) on your synthetic data and evaluate on real data, versus training on Euclidean-FM synthetic data. This directly validates the stated purpose and would be the headline result.
Formalize the geometry. Define M as the oriented blow-up of C at 0; introduce g_ρ = dA² + ρ²dθ² and ablate ρ; and upgrade the 36.3% conditional-path argument to a marginal-level statement — e.g., a proposition bounding target variance Var[u_θ^cyl] vs. Var[u^Euclid] under the coupling, which is the quantity that actually governs learnability.
Replace the Fisher hand-wave with a derivation: weight ∝ A² from the phase CRLB under complex Gaussian noise, and ablate A⁰/A¹/A² weightings.
Specify the sampler in an algorithm box, including exactly how the k-space DC projection (linear in C) is mapped into (A, θ) mid-integration, and whether this projection commutes acceptably with the cylindrical flow; state solver steps/NFE and wall-clock overhead vs. the baseline.
Fix statistics and presentation: per-volume clustered tests (or Wilcoxon over volumes), defined ± semantics, a per-slice ΔPSNR distribution plot with the median slice shown in Fig. 2, and unified CPE/GPE naming.
Broaden validation: a second dataset (e.g., fastMRI multi-coil brain with ESPIRiT-derived phase) and a sketch of the multi-coil extension — per-coil cylinders (ℝ⁺ × S¹)^C or a shared-phase factorization — would remove the single-dataset caveat.
Writing repairs: rewrite the π₁ paragraph (L66–67) so the causal logic runs the right direction; correct the polar-geodesic sentence (L65); temper "intrinsic manifold"; resolve SKM-TEA vs. SKM-TEA-mini; and cite the angular-geodesic generative precedents (torsional diffusion on tori, RFM on flat tori in Chen & Lipman) to position the novelty honestly as product-manifold FM + amplitude-aware weighting + MRI application — which, executed with items 1–2, would be a genuinely strong contribution.

Confidence: 4/5 — I verified the key analytic claims (attenuation constant, velocity bounds, curvature/topology statements) but have not run the code, which is not provided.
