\section{Methodology: Cylindrical Flow Matching}
\label{sec:method}

To construct a topology-aware generative model for complex-valued MRI data, we formulate continuous normalizing flows directly on the intrinsic manifold of the signal. A complex signal $z = A e^{i\theta} \in \mathbb{C}$ naturally resides on a cylindrical manifold $\mathcal{M} = \mathbb{R}^+ \times S^1$, equipped with the product metric $g = dA^2 + d\theta^2$. This deliberate choice decouples amplitude and phase transport: geodesics interpolate each component independently along the shortest path, preventing the amplitude collapse inherent to the isometric polar embedding $g_{\mathrm{polar}} = dA^2 + A^2 d\theta^2 \cong \mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, whose geodesics are straight lines through the origin.

While this manifold possesses a vanishing Riemann curvature ($K=0$), it exhibits a non-trivial first fundamental group ($\pi_1(\mathcal{M}) = \mathbb{Z}$). This non-trivial topology is the fundamental source of Euclidean phase collapse in standard diffusion models: interpolating phase in a flat space $\mathbb{R}^2$ ignores the $2\pi$-periodicity and forces paths through the singularity at the origin, destroying amplitude and inducing phase discontinuities. A significant computational advantage of our geometric formulation on $\mathcal{M}$ is that it is parallelizable; consequently, the exponential and logarithmic maps admit closed-form, $\mathcal{O}(1)$ analytic evaluations without the need for costly numerical ODE geodesic integrators.

\subsection{Continuous Network Embedding}
To circumvent artificial jump discontinuities at the branch cut $[-\pi, \pi)$, our network operates on a 3-channel continuous trigonometric embedding $x = (A, \cos\theta, \sin\theta) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ as input. The neural network then predicts a 2-channel velocity field $u_t = (v_A, v_\theta) \in T_{(A, \theta)}\mathcal{M}$, corresponding to the tangent space of the amplitude and phase.

\subsection{Loss Formulation}
Given paired samples $x_0 = (A_0, \theta_0) \sim p_0$ and $x_1 = (A_1, \theta_1) \sim q$, we construct geodesic probability paths along each component: $A_t = (1-t)A_0 + t A_1$ and $\theta_t = \exp_{\theta_0}^{S^1}(t\, u_\theta)$. The target velocity field on the product manifold is:
\begin{equation}
    u_A = A_1 - A_0, \quad u_\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(\sin(\theta_1 - \theta_0),\, \cos(\theta_1 - \theta_0))
\end{equation}
where $u_\theta$ computes the shortest signed angular difference via the $S^1$ logarithmic map, correctly resolving transport across the $\pm\pi$ boundary.

The network $v_\phi(t, x)$ is trained to regress the target velocity field $u_t(x_t)$ via an $L_1$ objective, which is specifically chosen to provide robust edge preservation in MRI images. Matching our implementation, the exact loss is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1} \left[ |v_A - u_A| + \lambda A_1 |v_\theta - u_\theta| + \gamma \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HF}} \right]
\end{equation}
Crucially, the phase loss is modulated by the target amplitude $A_1$ (with weight $\lambda$). This amplitude mask is mean-normalized across each batch to stabilize training gradients, and gracefully prevents gradient explosion in noise or air background regions where the signal phase is undefined.

Furthermore, we apply a spatial-frequency regularization term to enhance sharp anatomical features: $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HF}} = \mathbb{E} [ | \mathcal{F}(v_\phi - u_t) | \odot M(k) ]$, where the velocity error is transformed into the frequency domain via a 2D Fast Fourier Transform and penalized by a radial mask $M(k)$ that linearly increases towards the periphery.

\subsection{Base Distribution and Inference}
We construct our generative process from the base distribution $p_0 = \mathcal{U}([0,1]) \otimes \mathcal{U}(S^1)$, a uniform prior on the unit cylinder (clamped with $\epsilon = 10^{-6}$ for numerical stability at the origin). This ensures there is zero probability mass strictly at the origin singularity $A=0$, effectively sidestepping topological breakdowns.

During inference, we simulate the learned vector field to generate samples. Given the analytic formulation of our manifold geometry, we employ a 2nd-order Heun predictor-corrector numerical integration scheme to solve the probability flow ODE accurately and efficiently.
