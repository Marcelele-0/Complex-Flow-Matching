\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate Cylindrical Flow Matching on complex-valued MRI synthesis and downstream reconstruction using 2D axial slices from the raw $k$-space SKM-TEA-mini dataset \citep{desai2021skmtea} (single-coil, first-echo image space). Retrospective $4\times$ Cartesian undersampling is simulated using 1D phase-encoding masks with a central 24-line autocalibration signal (ACS) band. We compare our geometry-aware approach directly against the Euclidean baseline; the training and inference pipelines remain exactly the same for both methods.

As shown in Figure \ref{fig:results_visual}, the Euclidean model struggles with phase wrapping, leading to artificial signal cancellations and magnitude dropouts near tissue boundaries (clearly highlighted in the residual error maps). In contrast, our geometry-aware approach explicitly models the $S^1$ phase topology, which not only yields continuous phase fields but also prevents destructive interference, resulting in significantly sharper anatomical reconstructions in the magnitude domain (PSNR $24.47$ dB vs $24.13$ dB; SSIM $0.588$ vs $0.530$).

To evaluate phase fidelity, we compute the Circular Phase Error (CPE) in radians:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CPE}(\theta, \hat{\theta}) = \frac{1}{|M|} \sum_{i \in M} |\operatorname{atan2}(\sin(\theta_i - \hat{\theta}_i),\, \cos(\theta_i - \hat{\theta}_i))|
\end{equation}
where $M$ denotes a tissue mask thresholded at $5\%$ of peak amplitude to avoid background noise bias. Table \ref{tab:results_metrics} outlines descriptive quantitative results on the held-out test volume (SKM-TEA-mini, $N=512$ slices), where both methods enforce exact Data Consistency ($\mathrm{DC\ Error} < 10^{-6}$) via interleaved k-space projections.
