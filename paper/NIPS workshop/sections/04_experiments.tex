\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate Cylindrical Flow Matching on complex-valued MRI synthesis and downstream reconstruction using the comprehensive raw $k$-space SKM-TEA dataset \citep{desai2021skmtea}. We compare our geometry-aware approach directly against the Euclidean baseline. Crucially, the training and inference pipelines, including the underlying neural vector field architecture, remain exactly the same for both methods; the singular modification is the underlying manifold topology.

As shown in Figure \ref{fig:results_visual}, the Euclidean model struggles with phase wrapping, leading to artificial signal cancellations and magnitude dropouts near the boundaries. In contrast, our geometry-aware approach accurately recovers the phase signal, preserving fine-grained high-frequency details essential for downstream medical diagnostics. 

To properly evaluate phase reconstruction, we employ the amplitude-weighted Geodesic Phase Error (GPE), explicitly defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{GPE}(\theta, \hat{\theta}) = \frac{\sum_i A_i \cdot (1 - \cos(\theta_i - \hat{\theta}_i))}{\sum_i A_i}
\end{equation}
where $A_i$ is the ground-truth magnitude. This formulation correctly accounts for the cyclic topology of $S^1$. Naive Euclidean "Phase MSE" is invalid because it improperly penalizes topologically equivalent angles (e.g., $-\pi$ and $\pi$) with a maximum squared error of $(2\pi)^2$ instead of 0.
